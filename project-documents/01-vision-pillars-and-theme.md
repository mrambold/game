# Vision, Pillars and Theme

The north star of the game: the one-sentence hook, the three design pillars every feature is judged against, the target player, the theme and setting, and what the game deliberately is not. Finding the theme is part of this work, so this document also records the theme candidates we considered and why we picked one.

> **Status: WORK IN PROGRESS (2026-10-24).** Pillars, tone and target player are agreed. The theme is being chosen from a shortlist of three. Hook and "what it is not" are still open. Resume at [Next steps](#next-steps).

## Agreed so far

### Pillars

| # | Pillar | Means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Simple pieces, deep interactions | Every card fits in one line of text. Depth comes from how cards combine, not from rules text. |
| 2 | Combos you discover | Synergies emerge from simple rules. The player feels clever for finding them. |
| 3 | The payoff moment | Scoring is the climax: escalating numbers, loud and satisfying feedback. |

- "One more run" is a goal, not a pillar. It should follow from pillars 1-3 plus short runs.

### Tone and audience

- Tone: comedic. The search started with "cozy and whimsical plus comedy". The cozy restriction was dropped during the theme search, comedy stays.
- Target player: a mix of Balatro fans and casual players. Not aimed at deep-strategy players.

## Theme (in progress)

### Requirements

- A round and a rising score target feel natural in the fiction.
- Combos are intuitive: the player can *feel* from the fiction what goes together.
- Supports 4-6 unlockable deck families (like Balatro's decks), each with a distinct playstyle.
- The scoring moment can be spectacular in sound and visuals (pillar 3).
- Comedic.
- Not taken by an existing score-based Balatro-like.

### Shortlist

All three follow the same pattern: **score = base value x multiplier**, where both halves come from the fiction (the Balatro chips x mult idea, but felt instead of learned). Names are working titles.

#### A. Galactic Prank Show

- **Hook:** Every UFO sighting, crop circle and Bigfoot photo was staged by aliens for the galaxy's most-watched prank show.
- **Cards:** aliens and their pranks. **Deck families:** alien races.
- **Round:** one episode at an Earth location (farm, suburb, Las Vegas...).
- **Score:** galactic viewers.
- **Boss rounds:** famous "cases" (Roswell, Bermuda Triangle), or a debunker on site who cancels one card type.
- **Core rule:** Viewers = Fright x Footage.
  - Fright: the scares (lights in the sky, humming, power cut, floating cow, a Grey in the corn).
  - Footage: whoever captures it (teen with a phone, security camera, news van, shaky alien film crew).
  - Escalation: each scare scarier than the one before gives a growing bonus (horror-movie build-up).
  - Witnesses: each location deals humans with obvious personalities. Farmer is hard to scare; teen films everything; conspiracy podcaster amplifies but nobody believes him; cop panics big, then confiscates the camera.
- **Example round (farm):** flickering lights (Fright 10) -> cattle vanish (Fright 20, escalation x1.5) -> Grey in the cornfield (Fright 40, escalation x2) -> teen witness with phone plus Cephalopod film crew (Footage x3 x2).

| Family | Playstyle |
|---|---|
| Greys | Classic scares, strong escalation |
| Cephalopods | The film crew: footage multipliers |
| Blobs | Shapeshift, copy the last scare |
| Reptilians | Disguised as witnesses, create footage from the inside |
| Insectoids | Many cheap small scares, build escalation fast |

- **Scoring moment:** humans panic, flashlights sweep the field, viewer counter explodes across a galaxy map.
- **Gap:** no score-based alien deckbuilder found. Nearest: Invaders (defend humanity, high score, 3 reviews), Cardstronaut (base defense), Zet Zillions (satirical space combat).
- **Risks:** escalation order and witnesses are two extra layers on top of the hand. They must stay simple (pillar 1).

#### B. Kaiju Insurance Claims

- **Hook:** You are the monster. The city's insurer itemises every ruin, and the claims must keep climbing.
- **Cards:** monster moves and city pieces. **Deck families:** monster species.
- **Round:** one district rampage.
- **Score:** insurance claim in dollars.
- **Boss rounds:** the army arrives (big stomps blocked), a rival kaiju, an earthquake-proof district, fine print ("fire damage not covered").
- **Core rule:** Claim = Damage x Chain reaction.
  - Damage: the monster's moves (stomp, tail swipe, breath).
  - Chain: what the city does next. Tall things topple onto neighbours, gas stations explode, fire spreads, water plus power lines spark.
- **Example round (downtown):** stomp a parking garage (Damage 20) -> tail-swipe the office tower (Damage 40) -> it topples into the gas station (Chain x2) -> fire spreads to the fireworks shop (Chain x3). The claims adjuster faints.

| Family | Playstyle |
|---|---|
| Reptile | Raw damage, the classic |
| Fire-breather | Fire spreads to neighbours |
| Sea beast (tentacles) | Grab and throw buildings to set up chains |
| Giant moth | Many small hits, dust swarms |
| Aquatic | Floods; water plus electricity combos |

- **Scoring moment:** skyline collapses, roar, claim ticker counting up with a cash-register *ka-ching*.
- **Gap:** open. Only small jam games found (Card Attack Kaiju, Kaiju Krush'em). Kaiju-A-GoGo (2015) is city-smash strategy, not a deckbuilder.
- **Risks:** chain reactions suggest a spatial layer (buildings next to each other). It needs a simple form, e.g. a single row of buildings, not a full grid.

#### C. Ragtag Town Band

- **Hook:** A hopeless small-town band takes every gig it can get (weddings, funerals, the mayor's retirement party) and has to win over every crowd.
- **Cards:** musicians and what they play. **Deck families:** instrument sections.
- **Round:** one gig.
- **Score:** applause.
- **Boss rounds:** a funeral (loud cards penalised), a heckling rival band, a critic in the front row, a bride who hates brass.
- **Core rule:** Applause = Sound x Harmony.
  - Sound: each musician's notes and volume.
  - Harmony: how well they play together. A solo is weak, a duet better, the full band (melody + rhythm + bass) best. Same key and a crescendo add more.
- **Example round (wedding):** tuba (bass, Sound 15) + snare (rhythm, Sound 10) + clarinet (melody, Sound 20) -> full band x3 -> all in C x1.5. Grandma starts dancing.

| Family | Playstyle |
|---|---|
| Brass | Loud, big Sound, some crowds hate it |
| Percussion | Rhythm, retriggers (beats repeat) |
| Strings | Harmony multipliers, slow build |
| Woodwinds | Melodic chains, flexible key |
| Choir | More voices = more Harmony |

- **Scoring moment:** the music swells layer by layer, the crowd dances. Strongest audio payoff of the three.
- **Gap:** music deckbuilders exist (Power Chord, Battle Bands, Rogue Tempo) but all are combat or board games. No score-based one found.
- **Risks:** "music deckbuilder" sounds less fresh than it is, so the hook must lead with comedy. Layered music is a large audio workload for a hobby project.
